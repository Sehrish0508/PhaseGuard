Project Description

PhaseGuard is a machine learning model that identifies the type of fault occurring on a 3-phase power line — instantly, from live current and voltage readings, without needing a technician to manually inspect the line.


It's trained on real fault data and classifies six common fault types, from single-line faults to full three-phase faults.


Move the sliders below to simulate live readings and watch the model predict in real time — no button needed, predictions update instantly.


Note: current values reflect real sensor readings; voltage values are normalized (per-model convention), so they appear as small decimals rather than real volts.

Industry Impact

When something goes wrong on a power line today, workers often have to physically drive out and manually check different points along the line, one by one, until they find the problem. This can take anywhere from a few hours to a couple of days.


PhaseGuard does this same job in under a second — just from live current and voltage readings, no driving around required.

Time and cost saved for a single small transmission line:

Time Saved (per fault)

~3 hours

Cost Saved (per fault)

~$3,600

Applied across a mid-size regional utility (~100 grid stations, roughly 1,500 lines):

Time Saved Annually

45,000–67,500 hrs

Cost Saved Annually

$54M–$81M

That's equivalent to over 5 years of continuous, round-the-clock work saved every year.

For scale, power outages cost U.S. electricity customers over $121 billion in 2024 (U.S. Department of Energy). Faster, automated fault detection is one of several approaches the industry is exploring to help address that cost.
